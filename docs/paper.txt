Abstract
This paper presents the General Photon Flux Framework II (GPFF II), a unified
quantum-sector formulation for calculating the observed photon flux from astrophys-
ical sources. The framework embeds photon propagation into a nine-component
quantum state space, where modes are labelled by curvature sector a∈ {− 1,0,+1}
and speed branch i∈ {− 1,0,+1}. A modified dispersion relation (MDR) gov-
erns the energy of each mode, incorporating Lorentz-invariance violation (LIV)
corrections and a curvature coupling term. The observed flux is expressed as the
occupation-weighted sum of mode energies and group velocities, combined with the
full geometric prefactor accounting for luminosity distance, angular incidence, and
cosmological redshift. The framework is validated against two physical test cases:
the solar constant at Earth and the isolated neutron star RX J1856.5 −3754. In both
cases the flat/luminal mode ( a= 0,i= 0) yields results consistent with established
observations, providing a benchmark for the quantum-sector corrections.

1 Introduction
The quantitative description of light received from a distant astrophysical source requires
accounting for several physically distinct effects simultaneously: geometric dilution of
flux with distance, the energy carried by individual photons, the angular orientation of
the detector, and the cosmological stretching of photon wavelengths and arrival rates
in an expanding universe. In conventional treatments these effects are introduced in
separate contexts — radiometry handles geometric dilution and angular dependence,
special and general relativity introduce redshift, and statistical mechanics provides the
Planck spectrum — and their combination is often left implicit.
Near the Planck scale, the further assumption that all photons propagate identically
regardless of their energy or the local spacetime curvature may also break down. Lorentz-
invariance violating (LIV) frameworks, motivated by quantum gravity candidates such as
Loop Quantum Gravity and string-inspired models, predict modified dispersion relations
(MDR) in which the photon group velocity acquires energy-dependent corrections [ 1,2].
Similarly, in curved spacetime sectors, the effective photon propagation deviates from the
flat-space result.
GPFF II addresses both levels of description by assembling the geometric flux factors
and the quantum-sector structure into a single coherent equation. Photon modes are
labelled by a curvature sector index aand a speed branch index i, and the observed flux
is an occupation-weighted sum over all modes. The geometric prefactor — luminosity
distance [ 3], angular incidence, and cosmological redshift [ 4] — appears explicitly at the
outermost level, while the MDR and group velocity of each mode capture the Planck-scale
physics.
This paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 derives and motivates each component of
GPFF II. Section 3 presents two numerical test cases. Section 4 discusses limitations and
open questions, and Section 5 concludes.

the fraction of emission within this band is ≲70%. Second, the gravitational redshift of
the neutron star surface ( zg≈0.22, [11]) slightly modifies the relation between the emitted
and received spectrum. Correcting for both effects brings the GPFF II prediction into
consistency with the observed flux.
4 Limitations and Future Work
The following limitations of GPFF II are noted:
1.Monochromatic formulation.The core flux equation applies to a single momen-
tum mode p(equivalently, a single wavelength). Bolometric calculations require
integration over p, which modifies the effective redshift correction and group velocity
weighting.
2.Superluminal branch.The i= +1 branch with λ+1>0 formally yields v > c .
In GPFF II this is treated as a perturbative correction near the Planck scale. A
future version should either provide a physical grounding for this mode — for
example through tunnelling amplitudes or evanescent wave analogues — or impose
the physical constraintw +1,i→0.
8
3.Coupling parameters unconstrained.The LIV couplings λ±1and the curvature
coupling κare left as free parameters. Constraining these from LIV observations,
gamma-ray burst timing [ 1], or gravitational wave data is the subject of future work.
4.Point source and no absorption.The framework assumes an isotropic point
source with no interstellar absorption. Dust extinction and scattering, which can
significantly affect fluxes particularly at UV and X-ray wavelengths, are not included.
5.Mode mixing not implemented.A full quantum-mechanical treatment would
propagate the occupation weights wa,idynamically along the photon worldline via a
Hamiltonian, allowing mode mixing to be computed self-consistently. This is reserved
for future work.
